          About this prototype

          A design exploration: what Do Computer could look like if a game console from around the year 2000 had shipped a personal-agent app with a mascot. It is one page with one scripted conversation. Nothing is sent anywhere and nothing is saved.

          The moon button in the rail switches between a pale day look and a deep-blue night look. They are one design in two palettes. Everything works from the keyboard; the keys are listed along the bottom.

          What is real

          • The creature. It is the app's own code, drawn live: the same seed, colors and states (listening, thinking, working at a command, a file, a search or its memory, speaking, waiting, done), and the same egg and hatch. Only one copy moves at a time. Every other one is a still picture.
          • The layout. The rail of pages, the chat list behind a “Chats” button, the chat in the middle with one status line while it works, requests for your OK above the message box, and the agent's panel on the right.
          • The words. Page names, descriptions, button labels and status lines such as “Running on workshop: …” are the app's own wording. The list on the right is the one-entry-per-request log that is being built.
          • The numbers behind the stats. Memory really is limited to 2,200 characters and “About you” to 1,375. The temper bars read the values in the creature's seed that decide how it moves.

          What is only presentation

          • The stage with its turntable, the little 1-bit readout, and memory counted in “blocks” of 11 characters, which makes a full memory 200 blocks.
          • The agent's page as a character sheet, the agent switcher as save slots, the day count, the key hints along the bottom and the “now loading” moments.
          • The conversation, files, automations and usage figures are made up for the demo. Buttons on the other pages only change what you see.

          What I would carry into the app first

          1. The stage. Give the creature a place to stand in its panel: bigger, on a turntable, with its name plate under it and a small moment when a task finishes.
          2. The log as save entries. One entry per request, with its steps shown as colored blocks, so the kind of work is visible before you open it.
          3. The two palettes and the type. Pale by day and deep blue by night, italic condensed capitals for titles and buttons, and a rounded face for everything you read.
